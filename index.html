Robot building

Our robot was built from a custom 3D-printed chassis, a photoresistor line-sensing circuit, a motor driver, and an ESP32 microcontroller. We prototyped on breadboards first, then soldered permanent connections for competition.

  • 3D-designed and printed chassis and sensor mounts
  • Photoresistor array with light shield for track detection
  • L298N motor driver, DC motors, and battery pack
  • Potentiometer calibration circuit on the back of the robot

PID Control

How we tuned our line-following robot for each track

Our robot uses a PID controller to stay centered on the line. The proportional term (P) corrects steering based on how far off the line the sensors read. The integral term (I) reduces steady-state drift over time, and the derivative term (D) dampens oscillation so the robot does not overcorrect.

We kept I and D constant across all tracks (I = 0.07, D = 0.72) and only adjusted speed and P for each competition event. Drag race favored maximum speed on a mostly straight course, while freq sweep and loop prioritized accurate line following over raw speed.

Track Speed P I D Reasoning
Drag Race 100 56 0.07 0.72 Max speed on a mostly straight track with few turns — lowered P for stability at high speed
Freq Sweep 56 100 0.07 0.72 Prioritized line-following accuracy over speed; higher P for tighter correction
Loop 76 100 0.07 0.72 Moderate speed — fast enough to compete but slow enough to stay on the track reliably

Improvements & Changes

What we fixed and what we would do differently

During the final week before competition, our improvements focused mainly on repairing broken hardware. We resoldered the photoresistor circuit, rebuilt the light shield to reduce interference from ambient light, and replaced the motors when they stopped working before reinstalling them. We also resoldered key connections and rewired parts of the robot to make the overall build more dependable. With more time, we would have redesigned the chassis so components and wiring stay clear of the wheels, built a more stable light shield, and spent additional effort fine-tuning PID parameters for each track.
